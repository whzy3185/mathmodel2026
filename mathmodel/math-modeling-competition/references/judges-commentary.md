# Judges' Commentary: Reusable Signals

## Evidence base

COMAP directs teams to UMAP Journal contest issues and states that judges emphasize thought process, analysis, modeling approach, mathematical methods, testing/error analysis, sensitivity/stability, strengths and weaknesses. The official instructions also say the Summary Sheet carries substantial weight and should be written last with the most important conclusions. Historic commentary repeatedly treats the summary, assumptions, clear equations, labeled figures and trustworthy sources as triage signals.

## Frequently rewarded

1. All required components answered and connected.
2. A concise summary with approach, concrete numerical findings and recommendations.
3. Assumptions that are necessary, justified, and visibly used.
4. A model motivated from the problem rather than dropped into the paper.
5. Specific results, uncertainty, error/sensitivity/stability testing.
6. Clear exposition; equations integrated into prose; tables and figures that convey evidence.
7. Trustworthy resources cited where they are used.
8. Insightful interpretation and honest strengths/weaknesses.

## Frequently penalized

- Missing a problem requirement during triage.
- Summary that restates the prompt, hides the bottom line, or conflicts with the paper.
- Unrealistic assumptions with no rationale or no link to equations.
- Unlabeled graphs, external figures without location-specific citation, and figures without an explained takeaway.
- A narrative of activities instead of a reproducible model.
- Results without tests, sensitivity, stability, or limitations.
- Unreliable sources and reference lists disconnected from specific facts.

## Operational rubric

Before submission, a reviewer must locate in under two minutes: the main answer to every subquestion; baseline; primary equations; parameter sources; validation; sensitivity; limitations; and the exact figures supporting headline claims. If the reviewer cannot, rewrite for discoverability rather than adding models.
